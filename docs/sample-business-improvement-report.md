# Sample small-business research and improvement report

<small>Practice artifact · researched 12 November 2026 · not commissioned by or delivered to the business</small>

## Business reviewed

**Malizzi Cakes & Creamery** — Douglassville, Pennsylvania.

The business's public website describes custom cakes, wedding and birthday cakes, cupcakes, specialty treats, cannoli, macarons, and hand-scooped Hershey's ice cream. The homepage presents three customer paths: request a custom order, shop selected ready-to-order treats, or visit for ice cream. It lists a new shop location at 955 Ben Franklin Hwy W., Suite 5, Douglassville, PA 19518. [Official website](https://malizzicakes.com/home)

The public homepage lists Tuesday–Wednesday 10 a.m.–4 p.m., Thursday–Saturday 8 a.m.–“6m”, and Sunday–Monday closed. The “6m” text appears to be a typo, but this report does not silently correct it; the owner should confirm the intended closing time before changing any listing. [Official website](https://malizzicakes.com/home)

The site explains a three-step custom-order process: provide the occasion, date, and serving size; the bakery reviews details and confirms availability/design; then the customer picks up the finished order. It also displays customer testimonials. [Official website](https://malizzicakes.com/home)

## Executive assessment

The site already has a useful foundation: clear product categories, separate custom-order and ready-to-order paths, a visible location, hours, contact details, and an explanation of how custom orders work.

The strongest low-cost opportunity is not a redesign. It is to reduce uncertainty for a first-time customer: confirm the public hours, make the custom-order requirements and timing easier to understand, and measure whether visitors can complete the right next step on a phone.

This is a public-web review only. No owner interview, customer survey, analytics, Google Business Profile access, mobile device testing, conversion data, or sales records were available. Recommendations below are hypotheses to test, not findings of lost sales.

## Findings and recommendations

### 1. Confirm the hours shown on the homepage

**Observed:** The published Thursday–Saturday hours end with “6m”. [Official website](https://malizzicakes.com/home)

**Recommendation:** Confirm whether the intended time is 6 p.m. Then update the website and any owner-controlled business listings to the same confirmed hours, including holiday exceptions.

**Why it matters:** Google says complete and accurate business information, including hours and special hours, helps customers understand when they can visit and can support local relevance. It also says local results are primarily based on relevance, distance, and prominence; no one can request or pay for a better local ranking. [Google Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-en)

**Effort:** Very low, after owner confirmation.

**Measure:** Zero mismatches across the website and owner-controlled listings during a monthly check.

### 2. Make the custom-order preparation checklist explicit

**Observed:** The homepage asks customers to share the occasion, event date, and serving size, then says the bakery will review details and confirm availability/design. [Official website](https://malizzicakes.com/home)

**Recommendation:** Consider adding a short “Have these details ready” checklist beside the custom-order action:
- event date and desired pickup date/time;
- approximate servings or cake size;
- flavor and filling preferences;
- theme, colors, and reference images;
- dietary/allergen questions;
- best contact method.

Only include items the owner actually wants or is able to collect. State the real lead time, response window, deposit/payment terms, and change/cancellation rules only after the owner confirms them. Do not invent policies.

**Why it matters:** The existing process already requests event date and serving size. Making the complete preparation requirements visible before the form may reduce avoidable back-and-forth. This is a usability hypothesis, not a proven conversion improvement.

**Effort:** Low if the current site editor allows a text change.

**Measure:** Track incomplete inquiries and the number of follow-up messages needed to obtain required details, using an owner-maintained count with no sensitive customer information.

### 3. Keep the three buying journeys distinct and easy to reach

**Observed:** The homepage separates custom event orders, ready-to-order sweets, and ice cream visits. [Official website](https://malizzicakes.com/home)

**Recommendation:** Preserve these three paths. On a phone, test that each primary action is visible without excessive scrolling and that each button opens the correct destination. Use descriptive button labels such as “Request a Custom Cake”, “Shop Ready-to-Order Treats”, and “Explore Ice Cream” if they accurately describe the destination.

**Why it matters:** Google recommends useful, well-organized, people-first content and clear descriptive titles and links. [Google Search Central SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

**Effort:** Low; test first, then adjust only if a real usability issue is found.

**Measure:** Complete a three-path phone test and record pass/fail for each path. If analytics are already configured, compare clicks to each path before and after changes.

### 4. Review local-search information for accuracy and completeness

**Observed:** The website provides the business name, physical address, phone number, product categories, and operating hours. This report did not inspect the owner's Google Business Profile or other directory listings.

**Recommendation:** Have the owner verify that the Google Business Profile uses the same confirmed name, address, phone, hours, primary category, and relevant attributes. Add current photos of the shop exterior, product range, and creamery if the owner has suitable images and permission to publish them. Respond to reviews in the owner's own voice; do not buy reviews or offer incentives for positive ratings.

**Why it matters:** Google's guidance recommends complete and accurate profile details, review responses, and photos/videos. [Google Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-en)

**Effort:** Low to moderate, depending on current profile state.

**Measure:** Checklist completion and monthly review of profile performance data, if available.

### 5. Check search-result page titles and descriptions before changing SEO

**Observed:** This review did not inspect the website's HTML title tags, meta descriptions, indexing status, Search Console data, or search-result appearance. No claim is made that these are currently defective.

**Recommendation:** Use the free Google Search Console to inspect whether important pages are indexed and how people discover them, if the owner already has or chooses to set up access. Then review whether each important page has a unique, accurate title and description that names the product and location where useful. Do not rewrite URLs or add pages just to insert keywords.

**Why it matters:** Google says clear, unique, accurate titles can influence title links, and useful page content helps people and search engines understand a page. It also cautions that ranking improvements are not guaranteed and may take weeks to assess. [Google Search Central SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

**Effort:** Moderate; do the inspection before editing.

**Measure:** Record baseline Search Console impressions, clicks, and queries for the relevant pages, then compare after several weeks. Do not promise a ranking or revenue increase.

## Suggested 30-day low-cost test

| Period | Action | Evidence to keep |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–3 | Owner confirms hours, order requirements, and policies | Approved fact sheet |
| Days 4–7 | Phone-test all three homepage paths | Pass/fail checklist and screenshots, if desired |
| Week 2 | Make only owner-approved clarity fixes | Change log and before/after copy |
| Week 3 | Verify owner-controlled local listing details and current photos | Completed listing checklist |
| Week 4 | Review available inquiry counts and Search Console/Profile data | Baseline-versus-follow-up table |

Avoid changing several things at once if the owner wants to understand which change helped. A simple baseline is better than guessing.

## Questions that must be answered by the owner

1. What is the correct Thursday–Saturday closing time?
2. What minimum lead time and details are required for custom orders?
3. Which policies (deposit, payment, delivery, changes, cancellation, allergens) should be public?
4. Which online actions currently generate the most useful inquiries?
5. Are Google Business Profile and Search Console already configured, and is there a baseline period of data?

## Limitations

- This report uses public website content and general Google guidance only.
- The business was not contacted. No forms were submitted and no account was accessed.
- No sales, margins, order volume, conversion rates, customer demographics, or customer satisfaction data were available.
- No competitor price comparison or market-size estimate was performed.
- The recommendations are a sample deliverable, not proof of demand for a paid service and not evidence that Eve itself completed a live research task.
